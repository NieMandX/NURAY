# Hosek–Wilkie sky and sun

The viewer ports the published **Hosek–Wilkie 1.4a** model, not a sky-gradient approximation.

- Original authors: Lukas Hosek and Alexander Wilkie, 2012–2013.
- Research and download: https://cgg.mff.cuni.cz/projects/SkylightModelling/
- Archive: `HosekWilkie_SkylightModel_C_Source.1.4a.zip`; SHA-256 `743e81a7fcbed06408490a303dcf1315083d7988a11fc69608e66f6e5417f9de`.
- Source files in `vendor/` retain the full BSD-3-Clause notices. `data.js` contains their RGB sky, spectral direct-sun and limb-darkening coefficients.
- CIE 1931 matching functions (360–720 nm at 1 nm) come from https://github.com/mmp/pbrt-v4/blob/master/src/pbrt/util/spectrum.cpp, Copyright 1998–2020 Matt Pharr, Wenzel Jakob and Greg Humphreys, Apache-2.0; license in `vendor/PBRT-LICENSE.txt`.

`model.js` cooks the reference fifth-degree Bernstein fits and interpolates turbidity and ground albedo. The spectral sun is integrated against CIE XYZ and converted to linear sRGB; limb darkening and the 0.51-degree diameter are retained. A fixed 0.04 radiometric-to-scene scale applies equally to sky and sun. There is no per-elevation normalization. Exposure and sky intensity are explicit artistic controls, not physical properties.

Physical model parameters: turbidity 1–10, ground albedo 0–1, sun elevation/azimuth from the geographic heliodon. Independent Rayleigh/Mie coefficients are **not** parameters of this fitted model. It is a clear daytime sky model; below the horizon the viewer fades its horizon fit to black by −6 degrees, an explicitly approximate extension rather than a night/twilight simulation.

Raster uses the analytic directional sky, an order-2 spherical-harmonic Lambert convolution for diffuse illumination, an approximate roughness blend for reflections, and integrated solar irradiance. Ray tracing directly samples analytic sky radiance and the limb-darkened solar disc with matching mixture PDFs and MIS. Water uses the existing finite geometry and shared Ocean FFT/Seascape normals; reflected geometry is ray traced. Water uses a rough dielectric reflection/transmission BSDF and homogeneous volume transport; waves change shading normals, not the baked intersection mesh.

`web-preview/tests/fixtures/hosek-reference.jsonl` is output from the **original C implementation**, generated by the adjacent C program. It covers turbidity/albedo/elevation boundaries and interpolation, RGB sky and five spectral sun wavelengths. Run `node --test web-preview/tests/hosek.test.mjs` from the repository root. ## Viewer behaviour

Merged GLB mode now loads the entire scene for raster, path tracing and cascades. `prototype/tiling/build_merged_trace.py model.glb` builds a hash-validated optional BLAS cache directly from the GLB, with no source-tile geometry dependency. Four shared GPU arenas contain the exact packed vertices, reordered indices and BVH nodes; raster and rays reuse these buffers. All instance matrices are retained. A TLAS includes every static block and every instance regardless of camera position. A device that cannot fit the full arenas receives an explicit error rather than a partial scene. Tiled mode remains available separately with its nearby-tile budget.

Water exposes absorption colour, attenuation distance in metres, scattering colour, turbidity and IOR (default 1.333) in both raster backends and ray tracing. Absorption is `-log(linearRGB(colour))/distance`. Raster uses a uniform reference water column, independent of the sparse shoreline index used for foam; it is not measured bathymetry. Ray tracing does not use it: `water-transport.wgsl` and `transport.wgsl` implement air/water transmission with Snell refraction, exact dielectric Fresnel (including total internal reflection), GGX VNDF sampling, radiance eta-squared compensation, and MIS. Foam is a diffuse surface-coverage mixture rather than a body tint. At least half of non-foam samples follow reflection (all under total internal reflection), with matching PDFs/throughput and MIS weights; this reduces wasted absorbed paths without changing Fresnel energy. Geometric normals determine medium entry/exit and ray offsets; Ocean/Seascape wave normals determine reflection/refraction directions. Water intersections use the existing scene BVH, including submerged opaque objects, banks and water exits.

Each actual ray segment in water is attenuated by `exp(-sigmaA * metres)`. Turbidity gives a homogeneous collision rate `0.12 * turbidity` per metre; exponentially distributed free flights scatter isotropically, multiplying throughput by linear scattering colour. This is a homogeneous multiple-scattering random walk with explicit absorption, bounded by the existing path-depth limit and Russian roulette. Direct environment sampling at a water exit evaluates the refractive BTDF. Underwater diffuse surfaces continue through actual water boundaries instead of using straight transparent shadow rays or the air-only irradiance cache. Both reference and cascade-source rays use the same transport function.

The city dataset has water sheets and banks, **not surveyed bathymetry or closed underwater volumes everywhere**. No seabed is fabricated. A submerged miss is not treated as a view of the lower-hemisphere sky; scattering can continue in the open medium, or the path terminates when absorbed. Camera immersion is classified by the nearest upward water boundary while ignoring opaque bridges. This supports the map's river/lake sheets and closed test slabs, not arbitrary overlapping/nested liquid volumes. Boundary gaps and missing underwater geometry remain source limitations. Small wave displacement, directional phase functions, spectral dispersion and specialized caustic sampling are not included; caustic paths rely on ordinary BSDF sampling and can be noisy.

Reference equations: [PBRT v4 dielectric BSDF](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Reflection_Models/Dielectric_BSDF) and [volume scattering integrators](https://pbr-book.org/4ed/Light_Transport_II_Volume_Rendering/Volume_Scattering_Integrators). The implementation is written for this viewer's WGSL/BVH layout. Open `/tests/water-transport.html` for executable GPU checks (actual production shaders) and a split-depth checkerboard pool.

Frozen water supports progressive accumulation and temporal reconstruction. Animated water uses fresh one-sample frames and invalidates temporal history; in cascade mode the animated frames use the reference path integrator until paused, avoiding a stale radiance cache. Every water/light change invalidates accumulation. In ray tracing, shadow-map strength, screen-space contact AO and bloom are disabled rather than misrepresented as physical transport controls. Actual ray occlusion, indirect lighting and water reflections remain active.

The local review URL uses `assets-hosek-check`, a relative symlink to the current r13 catalog, so no city assets are duplicated. Its water/light preset is isolated from the main catalog's localStorage settings.
